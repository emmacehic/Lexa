Loanword and Code-Switching Annotation Guidelines

1. Aim of the annotation

This project collects spontaneous spoken language from bilingual and multilingual communities. The data come from naturally occurring speech, for example interviews, conversations, podcasts, community media or vlogs. The aim is to document how people actually speak in real situations and to identify:

• loanwords;
• code-switches;
• unclear or ambiguous cases.

The recordings are first transcribed automatically. This transcript is only a starting point. Annotators should listen to the recording, correct clear transcription errors and then annotate relevant words or expressions directly in the transcript.

The focus is on language in the wild. Informal, dialectal and non-standard language should therefore be preserved when it reflects what the speaker actually says. It should not be changed into standard written language.

2. Basic workflow on the website

For each recording:

1. Open the assigned recording from the Library.
2. Read the project-specific guidelines under Guidelines.
3. Listen to each transcript segment and check it against the recording.
4. Correct clear transcription errors by selecting the relevant text and choosing Correct.
5. Select a word or expression that should be annotated.
6. Choose Loanword, Code-switch or Unclear.
7. Complete the additional annotation fields where possible.
8. Check that the selected span and annotation information are correct.
9. Mark the segment as reviewed once you have checked both the transcript and its annotations.

The website saves the work automatically. The Saved indicator shows that the latest changes have been stored.

The playback controls can be used to play and pause the recording, move two seconds backwards or forwards, change the playback speed and move to a specific point using the seek bar. The timestamps can also be used to inspect individual segments.

If a word is difficult to hear, listen to the segment several times and also check the speech immediately before and after it.

3. Checking and correcting the transcript

Before annotating loanwords or code-switches, check whether the transcript matches the audio.

Please:

• correct clear transcription errors;
• keep repetitions, hesitations, fillers, false starts and self-corrections when they occur in the speech;
• keep dialectal, informal and non-standard forms as they are spoken;
• do not correct the speaker's grammar or word choice;
• do not translate words from another language;
• do not make the transcript more formal or fluent than the recording;
• use [inaudible] when a part cannot be understood reliably.

The transcript does not need to represent every small phonetic detail. The main goal is to represent the spoken content accurately while preserving relevant features of spontaneous speech.

Correction or non-standard speech?

Only use Correct when the written transcript does not match what the speaker actually says.

Do not correct a form simply because it differs from the standard language. A dialectal pronunciation, informal form or grammatical feature is not a transcription error if it accurately represents the recording.

When making a correction, enter the form that can actually be heard. The website keeps a record of the original and corrected versions.

4. Annotation labels

Loanword

Choose Loanword for a word that originally comes from another language but is used as part of the main language of the utterance.

A word is more likely to be a loanword if:

• it is commonly used in the main language or speech community;
• it follows the grammar of the surrounding language;
• it has local case endings, plural endings or other grammatical marking;
• its pronunciation is adapted to the main language;
• it behaves like a normal word in the sentence.

Example:

  Idemo za vikend.

Here, vikend can be annotated as a loanword because it is commonly used and behaves like a normal word in Bosnian/Croatian/Serbian.

A word does not need to fulfil every criterion. These are clues, not strict rules.

Code-switch

Choose Code-switch when the speaker temporarily changes into another language.

A code-switch can consist of one word, several words, a phrase or a complete clause or sentence.

Example:

  I onda sam rekla I don't know, šta da radim.

The complete expression I don't know should be selected and annotated as a code-switch.

A single foreign word can also be a code-switch. Do not automatically classify every single word as a loanword.

Unclear

Choose Unclear when there is relevant evidence for borrowing or code-switching but the distinction cannot be made confidently.

This may happen when:

• a single word is frequently used by bilingual speakers but is not clearly established as a loanword;
• pronunciation is difficult to judge;
• grammatical integration is unclear;
• the source language is uncertain;
• the audio or context does not provide enough evidence.

When using Unclear, briefly explain the problem in the note or comments field.

Words that do not need annotation

Words that are not loanwords, code-switches or relevant unclear cases should remain unmarked. There is no need to label ordinary words individually.

5. Deciding between a loanword and a code-switch

The distinction is not always clear. Consider the item together with the complete utterance and, where useful, the wider recording.

The following questions can help:

1. Is the word or expression originally from another language?
2. Is it commonly used as part of the main language?
3. Does it follow the grammar of the surrounding language?
4. Does it have endings or other morphology from the surrounding language?
5. Does its pronunciation sound local, foreign or mixed?
6. Does the speaker appear to switch temporarily into another language?
7. Are several words from the other language used together?
8. Does the expression retain the grammar of the other language?

No single question decides the label on its own. A word should not automatically be labelled as a code-switch simply because it looks English. It should also not automatically be labelled as a loanword merely because it appears in a dictionary.

Dictionaries and other reliable language resources may be used as additional evidence. If the available evidence remains mixed, choose Unclear instead of guessing.

6. Selecting the annotation span

Select exactly the word or continuous expression that belongs to the annotation.

For a loanword:

  Idemo za vikend.

Select only vikend.

For a code-switch:

  Und dann dachte ich what am I supposed to do now und bin gegangen.

Select the complete switched expression, not only one word from it.

Do not include surrounding punctuation or unrelated words unless they are genuinely part of the switched expression. If two separate items occur in the same segment, create separate annotations.

The website highlights annotated spans and automatically connects each annotation to its sample and timestamp. Annotators do not need to add annotation tags manually.

7. Additional annotation information

After choosing a label, complete the additional fields as far as the evidence allows.

Source language

Enter or select the language from which the loanword comes or the language into which the speaker switches.

If the source language is not known confidently, leave it blank or explain the uncertainty in the note instead of guessing.

Recipient or main language

The recipient language is the main language of the surrounding utterance. It is the language into which a loanword is integrated or from which the speaker temporarily switches.

The source and recipient languages should not normally be identical. If the website shows the same language on both sides, check the annotation again.

Native alternative

Add a natural alternative in the main language when one exists and is useful. It should fit the meaning in the sentence and sound natural. Leave the field blank if no clear alternative exists. Do not invent an unnatural translation simply to complete the field.

Pronunciation

Choose:

• local – the pronunciation sounds adapted to the main language;
• foreign – the pronunciation is closer to the source or switched language;
• mixed/unclear – it cannot be assigned confidently or contains features of both.

Pronunciation is supporting evidence only. A detailed phonetic analysis is not required.

Confidence

Choose:

• high – the label is clear and supported by several clues;
• medium – the label is likely, but some uncertainty remains;
• low – the evidence is weak or conflicting.

A low-confidence item does not automatically have to be labelled Unclear. For example, an item may probably be a loanword but receive low confidence because the audio quality is poor. Use Unclear when the distinction itself cannot be made responsibly.

Note or comments

Use the note for information that helps another annotator understand the decision, especially:

• why an item was labelled Unclear;
• relevant pronunciation or grammatical evidence;
• a quotation or deliberately marked expression;
• uncertainty about the source language;
• a difficult annotation boundary;
• information obtained from a dictionary or another source.

Notes can be short. They should explain the decision rather than repeat the label.

8. Special cases

Proper names

Names of people, places, organisations, companies, products or social-media platforms are normally not annotated only because they come from another language. Examples include Michael, London, Instagram and BMW.

If a name is part of a longer switched expression, annotate the expression according to its use in context. Mention particularly difficult cases in the note.

Abbreviations

International abbreviations such as EU, NATO or WHO are not automatically loanwords or code-switches. Consider how they are pronounced and used in context.

Adapted loanwords

A borrowed word may receive a local case ending, plural ending, verbal morphology or adapted pronunciation. These forms can still be annotated as Loanword. Always keep the form that the speaker actually produces.

Quotations

If the speaker quotes words that were said in another language, annotate the foreign-language part as Code-switch and write quotation in the note.

Fillers and discourse markers

Items such as okay, yeah or well can be difficult to classify. If the item is commonly used as part of the main language, it may be a loanword. If it clearly belongs to a temporary switch, label it as a code-switch. If the evidence is insufficient, use Unclear.

Dialectal and regional forms

Do not annotate a word as a loanword or code-switch only because it differs from the standard language. First consider whether it may simply be dialectal, regional, informal or an ordinary phonological variant.

If a dialectal explanation is plausible and there is no additional evidence of borrowing or switching, leave the word unmarked.

Technical and international vocabulary

Scientific, technical and international words should not automatically be treated as loanwords. Consider whether the project treats them as established vocabulary, recent borrowings or excluded international terms. Use the note for difficult cases and follow any project-specific instructions.

9. Segment and sample types

The website automatically derives the sample type from the annotations in a transcript segment:

• LOAN_ONLY: The segment contains at least one loanword and no code-switch.
• CS_ONLY: The segment contains at least one code-switch and no loanword.
• BOTH: The segment contains at least one loanword and at least one code-switch.

If a segment contains no loanword or code-switch, the sample type remains blank.

Annotators do not need to enter the sample type manually. Unclear is an item-level label and does not by itself make a segment LOAN_ONLY, CS_ONLY or BOTH.

10. Speaker and source metadata

Speaker and source information may include an anonymous speaker ID, main language or L1, other languages, dominant language, bilingual or heritage-language status, country or region, current country, generation, language used at home, speaker role, source context, source URL and metadata source.

Only enter information that is known from the source or has been provided reliably. Do not guess a person's age, gender, nationality, migration history, language proficiency or other personal information from appearance, accent or name.

Use anonymous speaker IDs rather than real names wherever possible. The same speaker should receive the same ID across recordings if their identity is known and the project permits this connection.

11. Reviewing a segment

Before marking a segment as reviewed, check that:

• the transcript matches the recording;
• spontaneous, dialectal and non-standard speech has not been unnecessarily normalised;
• every relevant item has been marked;
• each annotation span has the correct boundary;
• the label is appropriate;
• source language, pronunciation and confidence are completed where possible;
• unclear or unusual decisions are explained in the note;
• names or abbreviations have not been annotated without a reason.

A segment may contain no relevant item. It can still be marked as reviewed after the transcript has been checked.

Do not mark a segment as reviewed if the audio is too unclear to transcribe reliably, the segment is in the wrong language or an important decision still needs to be discussed.

12. Difficult and uncertain cases

Uncertainty is expected in this project. The distinction between borrowing and code-switching is not always categorical.

If you are unsure:

1. listen to the item and its wider context again;
2. consider grammatical integration and pronunciation;
3. check a reliable dictionary or another language resource if useful;
4. compare the item with similar examples already annotated in the project;
5. choose Unclear if the evidence remains mixed;
6. briefly explain the problem in the note.

It is better to document uncertainty than to make an arbitrary decision.

13. Short example

Transcript:

  Za vikend sam rekla I don't know, možda ostanem kući.

Annotation 1:

• selected form: vikend
• label: Loanword
• source language: English
• pronunciation: local
• confidence: high

Annotation 2:

• selected form: I don't know
• label: Code-switch
• source language: English
• pronunciation: foreign
• confidence: high

The website will classify this segment as BOTH because it contains at least one loanword and at least one code-switch.

The transcript itself should remain in its natural spoken form. The website displays the annotated spans visually and includes the annotations, corrections, timestamps and metadata in the corpus preview and export.
